Turbo, the car that teaches

Turbo is a car with feelings but no human body: no arms, no legs, no face painted on the windscreen. He sits in the passenger seat in spirit only; in practice he is the autoescuela car you learn in, with the L sign on the roof. Four ways to give him a personality:

Humanized, not humanoidEmotion comes from car parts: lights, bonnet, mirrors, suspension, wipers, horn.
Clear of CarsNo eyes in the windscreen and no mouth across the bumper, which is Pixar's look.
An invented modelNo real brand's shape (a SEAT 600 or a Mini), so there is no trade dress problem.
Readable at 40 pxHe has to work as a tiny face in the feedback sheet and as the app icon.

What a car Turbo changes in the flow

You drive Turbo

Every Spaniard learns in the autoescuela's car. Turbo is that car, so "Arrancar" literally starts him. On La Ruta, the vehicle moving along the road is Turbo, not a separate avatar.

Your avatar becomes the driver's side

The proposal had "Tu coche" as your avatar. With Turbo as the car, your identity moves to what you put in him: a keyring, seat cover, air freshener and dashboard figure, collected in the Guantera.

Stickers go on Turbo's rear window

Each mastered tramo is a sticker on his back window, so he fills up as you learn. Shareable cards show Turbo with your stickers.

The ending gets better

When you pass, Turbo takes off his L sign and hands you the keys: "Ahora conduces tú." You can pick your own first car as the last reward; Turbo stays as your co-driver for the novice year.

Sounds are free

Two short horn beeps for a right answer streak, a soft indicator tick while thinking, an engine purr on the Llegada. All synthesised, like Volantón.

Never sad, never broken

Same rule as Rumbo: no flat tyres, no breakdowns, no warning lights when you miss a day. A miss gets his curious tilt, nothing more.

Sketches only, drawn in code to compare directions. The final Turbo would be illustrated and rigged in layers, as Rumbo was.
