Your five questions, answered

Names, colours, one road or free choice, tips and tricks, and what goes in the Guantera. Each section ends with what I recommend.

1 Name check

Relanti looks clear; Turbo is crowded

Low risk so far

Relanti

Web searches found no app, company or trademark called Relanti, and nothing in our sector. The nearest hit is an unrelated US filing for "RELANT". The word is our own play on ralentí, which helps it stand out.

Crowded

Turbo

"Turbo" is registered in many classes, and there are driving schools using it right in our sector: Turbo Autoescuelas in Navarra and Autoescola Turbo in Catalonia. A US education trademark, "TURBO AI" (Turbolearn), also exists.

What I could not do from here: the official databases (EUIPO's TMview and OEPM's Localizador) block automated access, so this is a web check, not a register search. Before launch, someone should run "Relanti" and "Turbo" in TMview for classes 9 and 41, and check the domains.
Recommendation

Keep Relanti as the app name and register it early (OEPM national mark is about 150 €; an EU mark is about 850 €). Keep Turbo only as a character name inside the app, never on the store listing or the icon text. That's the same approach that saved Rumbo. If a school called Turbo ever objects, renaming a character is cheap.

2 Colour

What the research says, and three palettes

Popular colour psychology (blue means trust, red means danger) makes for good stories but has thin evidence. The best-known lab result, that seeing red lowers test scores, did not hold up: a 2020 meta-analysis of 38 samples found no reliable effect once publication bias was corrected (Gnambs, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review). So I'd choose colours for reasons that do hold up:

Meaning people already know

On the road, blue signs inform, red frames warn or forbid, yellow marks roadworks, and green is "go". Using those meanings in the app reinforces the exam content.

Contrast and colour blindness

About 1 in 12 men can't tell red from green easily. Right and wrong always get an icon as well as a colour, and all text meets WCAG 4.5:1.

Standing out

Most DGT test apps look like government forms. A warm, road-trip palette is a choice none of them makes, which helps the store screenshots.

3 One road or free choice?

A recommended road, with every exit open

You're right to worry. Someone tired of speed limits should be able to go and do vehicle types. The research supports both halves of the answer:

Choice helps motivation

A meta-analysis of 41 studies (Patall, Cooper & Robinson, 2008) found that giving learners a choice raises motivation, effort and performance, with the best results from a few meaningful options rather than many.

Mixing topics helps the exam

A meta-analysis of interleaved practice (Brunmair & Richter, 2019) found a moderate benefit overall, and the biggest one for telling similar-looking visual categories apart. That's exactly signs. The real exam mixes topics too.

But learn each thing once in order

The same research shows that new material is easier in blocks. Some rules build on others: you need the signs before priority at junctions.

Recommendation

One recommended road, but every etapa unlocked from day one. The road follows the order of the DGT's simplified manual, so it makes sense to anyone coming from an autoescuela. Tapping any stop takes a desvío, Turbo says "Recalculando ruta", and your place on the main road is kept. Inside an etapa, tramos go in order. Retrovisor reviews and simulacros always mix topics. If you do three rounds of the same etapa, Turbo suggests "¿Cambiamos de aires?" with two other etapas to choose from.

4 Tips and tricks

Trucos de Turbo: our edge over other apps

Other apps are mostly question banks. Relanti can teach how to read the exam, plus memory tricks for the numbers people forget. Each truco needs a source, and the exam-technique ones should be checked against real DGT questions before we ship them. Some first findings:

g/l
mg/l

El aire es la mitad de la sangre.

0,5 g/l sangre→0,25 mg/l aire

General limit: 0.5 g/l in blood, which is 0.25 mg/l in exhaled air. Novice drivers (under 2 years) and professionals: 0.3 g/l, which is 0.15 mg/l. To get the air number, halve the blood number.

Note: a proposal to lower the limit to 0.2 g/l passed its first vote on 29 Sep 2026 but lapsed when the Cortes were dissolved for the 29 Nov elections. This is exactly the kind of rule our content must track. Source: Motor16, 9 Oct 2026.
!

"Siempre" y "nunca" son señales de peligro.

Answers with absolute words are often wrong, because traffic rules have exceptions: an agent's instructions override a light, and a sign overrides the general rule. Look for the exception before choosing.

FOTO

La foto da el contexto; la pregunta decide.

This is your hypothesis, and I think it's worth testing. Many questions with photos ask about a general rule or a detail the photo doesn't settle. The tip would be to read the question first, then use the photo only for road type, weather and signs. I'd validate it against a sample of real DGT questions before Turbo says it.

≤

La velocidad máxima es un techo, no un objetivo.

Questions about rain, fog or a school exit often hide behind the speed limit. The right answer is usually "adapt", not the number on the sign.

A·B·C

Lee las tres antes de elegir.

The options often differ by a single word. Rushing and not reading carefully are the two most common mistakes. You have about 1 minute per question, which is plenty.

P·D·E

Detención, parada y estacionamiento no son lo mismo.

A detención is stopping because traffic requires it. A parada is up to 2 minutes without the driver leaving. Anything else is estacionamiento. Many questions turn on that word (definitions in the Ley de Tráfico annex; rules in RGC arts. 90 to 94).

NEW

Ojo con las novedades.

Data from over a million practice tests (Instituto Pons de Seguridad Vial) shows that people fail most on signs and on recent rule changes: 20 km/h on single-level streets, the minimum number of occupants in VAO lanes, and personal mobility vehicles. Relanti can mark these questions "Novedad".

Recommendation

There are three kinds of truco. Reading trucos teach how to read the exam. Memory trucos are mnemonics for numbers: alcohol, speeds, distances, ages. Novedades flag recent rule changes. Each one has a source, a "checked on" date, and the questions it explains, as in Volantón. A "Trucos de Turbo" library in the Guantera lets people revise them before the exam.

5 The collection

What do you bring back from a road trip?

Volantón had two levels: a stamp per flight, and a visa or passport cover for bigger things. Relanti needs the same: something small and plentiful for each tramo, and something bigger for each etapa.

Per tramo

Tickets de peaje

Each tramo you master gives you a toll ticket with its road code, date and km. They stack up clipped to the sun visor, the way everyone keeps them. They're small, plentiful and very Spanish.

Per etapa

Imanes de nevera

Finish an etapa and you get the magnet from that stop: Villaseñal, Faro Nocturno and the rest. They live on a fridge door at home, so the collection reads as "places I've been". Each magnet can open to show its trucos.

Per etapa, alternative

Llaveros

A keyring per etapa, all hanging from Relanti's key. It gets fuller as you learn, and when you pass, Turbo hands you that bunch of keys.

Also possible

Fotos del mirador

A polaroid at each stop, with your car at that place. It's personal and shareable, but needs art for every stop and car.

Also possible

Atlas de carreteras

A paper road atlas where your route is drawn in highlighter, with notes in the margin, like Volantón's «Lo que aprendí». It's nostalgic and makes a good memory note.

Also possible

Recuerdos de área de servicio

Funny souvenirs from motorway service stations: a fan, a little bull, a botijo. Great for humour, but avoid real brand silhouettes such as the Osborne bull, which is a trademark.

Recommendation

Toll tickets for tramos, fridge magnets for etapas. The Guantera becomes two things you open: the sun visor full of tickets ("38 de 96") and the fridge door of magnets ("5 de 12"). Your car's looks stay separate, as kit that Turbo fits at each rango. If you prefer the keyring, it could replace the magnets and ties in nicely with the final scene where you get the keys.
